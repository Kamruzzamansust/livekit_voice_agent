TRIP PLAN: SAINT MARTIN'S ISLAND (3 DAYS, 2 NIGHTS)

== Overview ==
Saint Martin's Island (locally called Narikel Jinjira, "Coconut Island") is the only coral island of Bangladesh, located in the Bay of Bengal about 9 km south of the Teknaf peninsula in Cox's Bazar district. It is famous for clear blue water, coral stones, coconut trees, fresh seafood and quiet sunsets. The island is small, about 8 square kilometres, and can be walked around in a few hours.

== Best Time to Visit ==
The tourist season is from November to January. Since 2024 the government limits tourism to protect the coral ecosystem: ships run only in this season, overnight stays are allowed only in December and January, and the number of visitors per day is capped (around 2,000). Visitors must register online through the Bangladesh Tourism Board before travelling. Rules can change every season, so always check the latest notice before booking. From February to October the sea is rough and ships do not operate.

== How to Get There ==
Step 1: Dhaka to Cox's Bazar. Options are an overnight AC bus (about 10 to 12 hours, 1,500 to 2,500 taka), a domestic flight (about 1 hour, 5,000 to 9,000 taka one way), or the Cox's Bazar Express / Parjotak Express train (about 9 hours, 700 to 2,000 taka).
Step 2: Cox's Bazar to Saint Martin. Tourist ships leave from the BIWTA jetty at Nuniyachora, Cox's Bazar, in the morning (around 7:00 AM) and take about 6 to 7 hours. A return ticket costs roughly 3,500 to 6,000 taka depending on the ship and class. The older Teknaf route is currently closed for security reasons.

== Day-by-Day Itinerary ==
Day 1: Arrive in Cox's Bazar early morning, take the ship to Saint Martin, reach the island in the afternoon. Check in to the resort, walk on the West Beach and watch the sunset. Dinner with grilled fresh fish (coral fish, lobster, crab) at the beach-side restaurants.
Day 2: Early morning visit to Chhera Dwip (Chera Island), the southern tip that becomes separate at high tide. Go by trawler or bicycle (rental about 100 taka per hour), or walk at low tide (about 2 hours each way). Return before noon. Afternoon on North Beach and the jetty area, try fresh coconut water and dried fish. Evening stargazing on the beach; the island has very little light pollution.
Day 3: Sunrise on the East Beach, buy dried fish or seaweed snacks, take the afternoon ship back to Cox's Bazar, then the night bus or train to Dhaka.

== Where to Stay ==
Budget cottages and guesthouses: 1,500 to 3,000 taka per night.
Mid-range resorts (for example near West Beach and North Beach): 3,000 to 7,000 taka per night.
Premium eco-resorts: 7,000 to 15,000 taka per night.
Book early for December and January, since visitor caps make rooms scarce.

== Food ==
Must-try: grilled coral fish, lobster, crab, fried rupchanda, coconut water and dried fish. A seafood dinner costs around 400 to 1,500 taka per person depending on what you order. Electricity is mostly from solar and generators, so expect limited power at night.

== Estimated Budget (per person, from Dhaka) ==
Budget trip: about 9,000 to 12,000 taka.
Comfortable trip: about 15,000 to 22,000 taka.
This includes Dhaka to Cox's Bazar return transport, ship tickets, two nights of stay and food.

== Packing List ==
Light cotton clothes, sunglasses, sunscreen, hat, sandals, power bank, torch, basic medicine (especially for sea sickness), a printed copy of the ship ticket and the tourist registration, and some cash because there are few ATMs and card payment is rare.

== Rules and Safety Tips ==
Do not collect or break corals, shells or any living sea creatures; it is illegal and damages the island.
Do not use single-use plastic; carry your waste back.
Loud music, bonfires and beach barbecues on the beach at night are discouraged to protect nesting sea turtles.
Do not swim far from shore; currents can be strong. Follow the lifeguards and local warnings.
Keep the ship's return time in mind; missing it means waiting for the next day.
